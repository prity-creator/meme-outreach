# DM Rules

The meme does the work. The DM only opens the door.

Send the meme first, then **exactly two short messages**, separated by `[new message]`:

1. **One light line tying the meme to them.**
2. **One casual, curious question.**

Nothing else: no proof points, no "compare notes", no call ask, no calendar link, no pricing, no pitch. Those come after they reply.

## Message 1 — the tie-in line

- One sentence, conversational, with one filler word (honestly, kinda, tbh, ngl) unless the brief says the sender writes formally.
- Ties the meme to something true about them: something they said, a season, a job post, their industry.
- An emoji at the end is fine if the brief's voice allows it (😅 works well).

**First message** (cold, nothing sent before): give the reason it made you think of them. Never pretend you've talked before.
> Saw you're hiring two PPC folks at once, so this one felt honestly a little relevant 😅

**Follow-up** (they went quiet): you can acknowledge the silence lightly, at most. Never guilt.
> Black Friday's about a month out so this one felt honestly a little too real 😅
> Pretty sure my last message got buried, so figured this might land better 😅

## Message 2 — the casual question

Ask it the way a friend in the industry would over coffee. It must not sound like a sales discovery question.

- Short: 18 words max.
- Plain words. No jargon like "push", "strategy", "goals", "bandwidth", "priorities", "solutions", "currently".
- Don't use their company name ("for Acme") — say "you guys" or "your team".
- Ask about their world, never about our service. Nothing that hints at a pitch.
- Easy to answer in a few words. Lightly playful is good.

| Sounds salesy | Sounds casual |
| --- | --- |
| "Is BF/Cyber Week the biggest paid search push of the year for <Company>, or is it spread across all the sale events?" | "Is Black Friday the big one for you guys, or is the whole year kinda one long sale?" |
| "Is the team covering those accounts in the meantime, or is it mostly landing on you?" | "Who's been stuck covering the extra accounts, you?" |
| "How are you currently handling paid search coverage?" | "Still running the ads yourself these days?" |
| "What are your top marketing priorities for next year?" | "Is next year's budget a fun conversation or a scary one?" |
| "Would you be open to exploring how we could help?" | (delete it — never in this DM) |

## After they reply

That's when the next message can move things forward, using the brief's "next step" and voice. A pattern that works well: answer what they said like a person, then offer to catch up this week or next to compare notes, with **one real proof point from the brief** ("we've been extra hands for teams like X"). People compare notes with people who've proven something. Never invent a proof point.

## Channel notes

- **LinkedIn connection requests can't carry images.** For a "first message" on LinkedIn to someone you're not connected with, the meme goes in the first DM after they accept; the connection note stays plain text.
- **LinkedIn DMs and email** both take an image. In email, put the meme in the body (not as an attachment) and use the two lines as the whole email, with a short, lower-case subject line like "this felt relevant".
